# PLAN - Identification time and the difficulty of citizen-science observations for biodiversity foundation models

Date: 2026-11-21
Stage: setup

## Working claim [H]
Within species, citizen-science observations that the community takes longer to identify are substantially harder for biodiversity foundation models that never saw them. Because training and evaluation sets are taken from snapshots of identified observations, these sets under-represent hard observations and overstate the accuracy on observations that actually arrive.

## Labels
Every statement in this plan carries one label:
- [E] established or measured, with its source;
- [R] reasonable interpretation;
- [H] hypothesis that needs a test;
- [U] unknown.

## Data-use rules
- Observer halves: an observer is locked if the SHA-256 digest of the ASCII text "IG8-half:" followed by the decimal observer_id, read as an integer, is odd; the observer belongs to the development half otherwise (implemented in `src/halves.py`). No photograph, identification history, model output or other outcome of a locked observer is downloaded, read, computed or used, except in a registered confirmatory decision of this plan, and then once. Observer ids of locked observers are read only for counting checks that a task asks for explicitly.
- The results of the earlier idea-gate project (G-IG8 to G-IG11, development half) are exploratory.
- Confirmatory claims rest only on the locked half and on new families, registered here before use and used once.
- The observer is the unit of independence in splits, matching and bootstraps.

D1 · 2026-11-21 · pre-specified; exploratory (development half, already examined in G-IG11.0)
Question: does the within-species gradient of accuracy with identification time remain among observations whose observer did not use the platform's computer-vision suggestion for the first identification?

Estimate: Δ = accuracy of fast minus accuracy of slow observations, in percentage points, with the definitions of identification time, within-species median split, ties and pooling of G-IG11.0. R2 reduced: lower bound of Δ(V0) > 0 and Δ(V0) < 0.5 × Δall.

D2 · 2026-11-21 · pre-specified; exploratory (development half, already examined in G-IG11.0 and D1)
Question: how much of the within-species gradient of first BioCLIP with identification time remains after adjustment for region, season, observer activity, photograph format, vision assistance, queue load at creation and regional identifier availability?
Data: the D1 rows (13,210 member observations, 628 species, development half), with the fast/slow labels of D1 on all rows.
Outcome: correctness of first BioCLIP (0 or 1). Linear probability models fitted by least squares with fixed effects; the coefficient b of the indicator "fast" (fast minus slow, in percentage points) is reported.
Models:
M0: species fixed effects + fast.
M1: species × 10-degree cell fixed effects + fast.
M2: M1 + quarter of creation + observer-activity stratum (S0 to S3) + photograph format (width and height of the first photograph, number of photographs of the observation) + vision stratum (indicators for V1 and VX, V0 as reference, as defined in D1).
M3 (primary adjusted): M2 + queue load + identifier availability.
M4 (descriptive only, never the adjusted estimate): M3 + number of identifications, number of distinct identifiers and any disagreement on the observation. These arise during identification and can be consequences of difficulty.
Covariates: queue load = log(1 + number of observations in the snapshot of the same family and the same 10-degree cell created in the same ISO week as the focal observation); if the snapshot holds no creation date, observed_on is used for both the counts and the focal week, and the report says so. Identifier availability = log(1 + number of distinct users other than the observer who identified observations of the same family and cell in the copied identification histories, excluding the focal observation). If a covariate cannot be built from the copied tables or the snapshot, the model is fitted without it and the report says so; the rule is applied to M3 as fitted.
Rows: M0 to M4 use the same rows (all D1 rows with every available covariate).
Intervals: 95% percentile intervals from a cluster bootstrap by observer (snapshot observer id), seed 20261121; 2,000 replicates for M0 and M3, 500 for the other models and the sensitivities; fixed effects are re-estimated in each replicate.
Rule (first BioCLIP):
A1 mostly remains: lower bound of b(M3) > 0 and b(M3) ≥ 0.5 × b(M0).
A2 partly remains: lower bound of b(M3) > 0 and b(M3) < 0.5 × b(M0).
A3 not detected after adjustment: lower bound of b(M3) ≤ 0.
Meaning for the next step: A1 - identification time is kept as a marker of difficulty, and C3 reports the adjusted share; A2 - the claim states an availability component, and C1 is registered with the M3 adjustment; A3 - identification time is treated mainly as a marker of identifier availability and region, and the claim is reframed before registration.
Sensitivity (reported, not part of the rule): M0 on the rows that keep within-cell variation in M1; M0 to M3 within V0 only; M0 to M3 without Columbidae; M3 with log(1 + identification time in days) in place of the fast indicator; M0 to M3 for BioCLIP 2 and BioCLIP 2.5 Huge (descriptive; they saw these photographs).
Descriptive: numbers of rows, species, cells and observers that keep within-group variation in each model; distribution of queue load and identifier availability; within-species correlation of identification time with each of the two.

D6 · 2026-11-21 · pre-specified; exploratory (development half only: the D4 sample of window W, and new identification histories of window W2 without photographs)
Question: (a) how large is the within-species gradient of C1 in window W; (b) which phase-1 sizes and phase-2 fractions reach the power targets of the confirmatory plan within its budget; (c) do the weighted estimators and the observer-cluster bootstrap keep their bias and coverage under two-phase sampling?
Definitions (confirmatory plan, draft 2):
- T: time from created_at of the observation to the first moment at which it is research grade with its snapshot species, reconstructed as in D4; days with fractions, UTC; identifications created after 2026-10-27 are ignored.
- Slow: research grade today with T of at least 1 day; fast: research grade today with T below 1 day.
- Strata: A0 (T < 1 day), A1 (1 to under 30 days), A2 (30 to under 365 days), A3 (365 days or more) among research grade; B2 and B1 among eligible needs_id, with label support as in D5.
- Weight: the inverse of (phase-1 fraction × phase-2 fraction).
- Δ and ER (species level): in each family, E_slow is the weighted mean error of slow observations and E_ref the weighted mean, over the same slow observations, of the weighted mean error of fast observations of their species; species without a fast or a slow observation are dropped and their share of slow weight is reported. Δ = 100 × mean over families of (E_slow − E_ref); ER = sum over families of E_slow divided by sum over families of E_ref.
- O, R and G_sup at S = 2025-01-01: as in D4 and D5, with the weights; equal family weights.
Part 1 (gradient in W): on the research-grade D4 sample (weights 1), for the three models: Δ and ER; A1, A2 and A3 each against A0 with the same estimator; the slope of error on log(1 + T) (linear probability model with species fixed effects); per family and pooled; observer-cluster bootstrap, 2,000 replicates, 95% percentile intervals. Shares of A0 to A3 among research grade and of B1 and B2 among needs_id, per family, scaled to the W population counts of D4.
Part 2 (W2 shares): window W2 = observations of the eight families with observed_on from 2025-01-01 to 2025-03-31 and all photo ids above 367,752,438, development half, frame and eligibility as in D4; per family, a simple random sample of 1,000 observations (seed 20261121) with research-grade and needs_id observations in proportion to the frame; identification histories from the public API; the same shares as in Part 1. No photograph is downloaded.
Part 3 (power; BioCLIP 2.5 Huge): simulated studies drawn from the D4 sample by resampling development observers with replacement, separately for the research-grade and the needs_id parts in proportion to the W population counts of each family, then applying the phase-2 fractions; 1,000 simulated studies per design. A study rejects if its estimate minus 1.645 × SD is above 0, where SD is the standard deviation of the simulated estimates of that design; it is strong if the same lower bounds of Δ and ER reach 1.0 and 1.15. Smaller true effects are represented after simulation, without new draws: the simulated Δ, O and G_sup are lowered by (1 − k) × their development estimate and E_slow is moved toward E_ref in the same proportion, for k = 0.5, 0.75 and 1. Designs: eight families in W, n1 per family in {8,000; 12,000; 16,000}; W2, n1 in {8,000; 12,000}, with the stratum weights rescaled to the W2 shares of Part 2; new families, n1 in {6,000; 10,000; 14,000}, simulated as six of the eight families drawn at random in each study, with half the effect; each with f_A0 in {0.3; 0.5} and f_B1 in {0.3; 0.5}.
Targets: H1 power to reject at least 0.95 and power to be strong at least 0.80 at k = 0.75; H3 and H4 power to reject at least 0.90 at k = 0.75; H5 the targets of H1; H2 power to reject at least 0.80 at k = 0.75 of the halved effect.
Choice: for each set (W, W2, new families), the design with the fewest phase-2 photographs that meets its targets; if none does, the largest design of the grid. The total number of phase-2 photographs and the achieved powers are reported; if the total exceeds 170,000, the choice is left to the chat.
Part 4 (bias and coverage): a synthetic population per family made of 20 copies of every development observer of the D4 sample, each copy with a new observer id; 400 two-phase samples with n1 = 1,000 per family, f_A0 = 0.4 and f_B1 = 0.3; in each, Δ (BioCLIP 2.5 Huge), O and G_sup with the observer-cluster bootstrap, 400 replicates, 95% percentile intervals. If Part 4 is estimated above 30 minutes, 300 samples and 300 replicates are used. Reported: bias (mean estimate minus population value, divided by the standard deviation of the estimates) and coverage, per quantity.
Rule (Part 4): BC1 adequate: coverage at least 0.93 and absolute bias at most 0.1 for all three quantities; BC2 not adequate: otherwise.
Meaning for the next step: BC1 - the observer-cluster percentile bootstrap is kept in the registration; BC2 - the interval method is revised (for example a bootstrap stratified by family and phase-2 stratum) and checked again before registration.

D6 addendum 1 · 2026-11-21 · clarifications and one correction, written before any computation of D6 (no API request, estimate or simulation made; registration commit 0d0ee8b)
1. Targets by set. Set W (eight families, window W): H1 is Δ (reject) and Δ with ER (strong); H3 is O; H4 is G_sup; a design meets the targets of set W only if the targets of H1, H3 and H4 are all met. Set W2: H5 is Δ (reject) and Δ with ER (strong), with the targets of H1; O and G_sup are not used. Set of new families: H2 is Δ (reject).
2. Correction of the rejection level. In Part 3 a study rejects if its estimate minus 2.326 × SD is above 0 (one-sided 0.01, the Bonferroni level over five hypotheses, so the computed power is a lower bound for Holm's procedure). A study is strong if it rejects, Δ minus 1.645 × SD(Δ) is at least 1.0, and ER minus 1.645 × SD(ER) is at least 1.15. SD is the standard deviation of the simulated values of that design.
3. Phase-2 fractions: A0 is subsampled with f_A0 and B1 with f_B1; A1, A2, A3 and B2 are kept whole; needs_id observations with no supporter count as B1.
4. Phase-1 draws in Part 3: within each family and each part (research grade, needs_id), development observers are drawn with replacement, each with all its rows of that family and part, until the row target is reached or first exceeded; the realized sizes are reported.
5. Smaller effects: in each family, E_slow is lowered by (1 − k) × the development value of (E_slow − E_ref) of that family; O and G_sup are lowered by (1 − k) × their development values. For the new families the factor is 0.5 × k, so H2 at k = 0.75 is evaluated at 0.375 times the development effect.
6. W2: within each family, the weights of each stratum are multiplied by the W2 share divided by the W share of that stratum; the expected phase-2 photographs use the W2 shares.
7. Part 4: the phase-1 sample is a simple random sample of the synthetic population, so research-grade and needs_id rows appear in the proportions of the D4 sample. The population value of each quantity applies the estimator's own formula to the whole synthetic population with weight 1; for O and G_sup the mixing weights of D4 and D5 (W population counts per family) are the same in the population value and in the estimates.
8. Research-grade observations that are never reconstructed as research grade (T undefined) are left out of Δ, ER and the strata, and counted per family.
9. All random draws of D6 come from one generator with seed 20261121, used in the order of Parts 1 to 4.
D6 addendum 2 · 2026-11-21 · post hoc, written after the D6 report was read
1. Power of H2 recomputed. The confirmatory interval of H2 resamples observers within six fixed families, so the variation from the choice of families is not part of it. For each of the 28 sets of six of the eight families and each of the 12 designs of the new-family grid, 200 simulated studies are drawn as in Part 3, with the effect factor 0.5 × k of addendum 1. SD is the standard deviation within the same set and design. A study rejects if Δ − 2.326 × SD > 0 and is strong as in addendum 1.
